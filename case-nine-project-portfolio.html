Case 01 · Delivery & Governance

Making a nine-project portfolio predictable

I owned planning and delivery across nine concurrent client projects and moved the account off reactive firefighting onto predictable delivery, lifting on-time completion to 93% and taking 28% off delivery timelines.

The challenge

When I picked up the portfolio, nine client projects were running in parallel and each was being managed in a standard way, despite the clients being spread across different parts of the world. There was no shared way to see which projects were healthy, so problems tended to land as client escalations instead of being caught early.

The real issue was that nobody could clearly see the dependencies between projects and teams. With limited regular communication across Infra, DevOps and ETL, we were often scrambling to respond to whatever surfaced that week, and my job was to cut through the clutter and bring structure to each part of the delivery process.

The approach

01

Managed each project around its own requirements

I ran each project based on its specific requirements and dependencies, adapting the approach where needed rather than forcing every project into the same process.

02

Gave the client and delivery teams one picture

I became the single point between the client and the engineering teams and kept scope, specs and UAT readiness in shared documents, so decisions got made off the same information instead of competing versions of it.

03

Made communication a regular part of delivery

I organized regular, short calls within my organization to track progress and surface issues. Where needed, I coordinated calls with the relevant client teams to keep things moving and avoid delays.

04

Turned reporting into a decision tool

I standardised how status and escalations were shared so leaders on both sides could see a slippage coming and choose how to respond, well before a milestone was at risk.

Takeaway

What changed the account was not any one process. It was getting everyone onto the same honest view of where things stood, early enough that we could still act on it. Once the client trusted our dates, the rest of the relationship got easier too.
