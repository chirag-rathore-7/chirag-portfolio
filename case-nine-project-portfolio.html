Case 01 · Delivery & Governance

Making a nine-project portfolio predictable

I owned planning and delivery across nine concurrent client projects and moved the account off reactive firefighting onto predictable delivery, lifting on-time completion to 93% and taking 28% off delivery timelines.

The challenge

When I picked up the portfolio, nine client projects were running in parallel and each was being managed on its own terms. There was no shared way to see which projects were healthy, so problems tended to land as client escalations instead of being caught early.

The real issue was that nobody could forecast the account. The client could not plan around our dates, the delivery teams across Infra, DevOps and ETL were reacting to whatever was loudest that week, and my job was to make delivery something people could actually rely on.

The approach

01

Ran the portfolio as one thing, not nine

I put every project on the same planning approach so I could compare them at a glance and see where the account as a whole was exposed, rather than tracking nine separate stories.

02

Gave the client and delivery teams one picture

I became the single point between the client and the engineering teams and kept scope, specs and UAT readiness in shared documents, so decisions got made off the same information instead of competing versions of it.

03

Made risk a conversation we had early

I kept the RAID log honest and worked it on a fixed weekly rhythm with named owners, so we were talking about problems while there was still time to do something about them.

04

Turned reporting into a decision tool

I standardised how status and escalations were shared so leaders on both sides could see a slippage coming and choose how to respond, well before a milestone was at risk.

Takeaway

What changed the account was not any one process. It was getting everyone onto the same honest view of where things stood, early enough that we could still act on it. Once the client trusted our dates, the rest of the relationship got easier too.
